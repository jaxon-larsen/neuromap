1. We said that the Context-free languages are a proper subset of the Context-sensitive languages. However, many Context-free grammars are contracting. How can any Context-free grammar be made non-contracting? (Hint: Chomsky Normal Form)
To be contracting is to have right-hand rules be shorter than left-hand rules in a grammar. When converting to CNF, we remove all rules with lambda, which goes a long way to ensuring no contraction happens, because changing anything non-lambda into lambda contracts it. You also restructure the rules, only allowing left-hand variables to go to either two variables or one terminal, ensuring no contraction.
