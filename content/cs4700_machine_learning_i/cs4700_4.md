### 2. Categorical Data Encoding
- Create a DataFrame with both categorical and numerical data.
- Apply `OneHotEncoder` to a categorical column.
- Apply `OrdinalEncoder` to a different categorical column.

**Task:** Compare the outputs of `OneHotEncoder` and `OrdinalEncoder` when applied to the categorical columns. Which method is more appropriate for nominal data?

Answer: Overall, one hot encoding seems to be a better option for nominal data. Ordinal encoding can cause the model to believe that certain options are "above" or "below" others because they are symbolized by a higher or lower number. In some circumstances, that's appropriate, like in small/medium/large sizes of clothes. However, ordinal data is inherently unranked, and a one hot encoding method makes it easier for models to parse without introducing bias.

## Part 2: Feature Scaling

### 1. Standardization and Normalization

- Standardize and normalize a dataset (e.g., the Wine dataset).
- Use `StandardScaler` and `MinMaxScaler` to scale the dataset and observe the differences.

**Task:** Explain the difference between standardization and normalization and their impact on the training process.

Answer: Normalization rescales data into a certain range, but keeps the distribution shape. Normalization does not protect against outliers, but helps bound the data, which is really helpful for certain applications. Standardization remaps the data onto a normal distribution, which is useful for something like linear regression, but does not guarantee boundaries like normalization does.
