Qubits hold a state, a vector on a [[Bloch Sphere]].
The state is not just at the poles as $\vert{}0\rangle$ or $\vert{}1\rangle$, but in a continuous [[Superposition]] across the entire sphere.
This allows them to encode probabilistic information.
Measuring the state of a qubit causes it to snap out of superposition, produces a classical bit (0 or 1), and simultaneously projects the qubit into the corresponding quantum state ($\vert{}0\rangle$ or $\vert{}1\rangle$)
$n$ qubits are dimension $2^n$
