## 9.1 Wireless characteristics
Attenuation: transmission's loss of strength
- Free space path loss (FSPL): radio wave moving far away
- Absorption: moving through materials
- Interference: at least two devices using the same technology
- Noise: at least two devices using different technology
Radiation pattern: orientation of radio waves from antenna
Polarization: physical orientation of antenna relative to Earth's surface
Gain: event enhancing transmission quality
Loss: event diminishing transmission quality
Decibel (dB): comparison of two electrical power measurements
RF metrics
- Decibels with reference to one milliwatt (dBm): comparison of dB to a reference value of 1 mW
- Equivalent isotropically radiated power (EIRP): theoretical amount of power generated by a wireless transmission source
- Received signal strength indicator (RSSI): graphical representation of a received wireless transmission
- Noise floor: baseline level of RF background noise and interference present in the wireless environment, measured in dBm

## 9.2 WLAN standards
IEEE implements standards, the Wi-Fi Alliance certifies devices meet the standards
2.4 GHz band: lower frequency, longer wavelength band used in a WLAN
Channel width: distance between starting and ending RF, measured in MHz
Channel overlap: interference type caused by nearby WAPs using the same channel or an overlapping channel
Bonded channel: aggregation of at least two adjacent channels
5 GHz band: higher frequency, lower wavelength; 25 channels that are 20 MHz wide
Unlicensed national information infrastructure (U-NII) band: defined by the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) for WLAN and wireless ISP use
- U-NII 1: band that includes 5 GHz channels 36, 40, 44, and 48
- U-NII 2/U-NII 2A: includes 5 GHz channels 52, 56, 60, and 64
- U-NII 2 extended/U-NII 2C: includes 5 GHz channels 100 to 144
- U-NII 3: includes 5 GHz channels 149 to 165
6 GHz band: higher frequency, lower wavelength; 59 channels 20 MHz wide
- U-NII 5: channels 1 through 93
- U-NII 6: channels 97 through 113
- U-NII 7: channels 117 through 181
- U-NII 8: channels 185 through 233


## 9.3 802.11 technical standards
IEEE Wi-Fi (B A G N AC AX BE) standards:
- 802.11b/Wi-Fi 1: 11 Mbps bandwidth on the 2.4 GHz band
- 802.11a/Wi-Fi 2: 54 Mbps bandwidth on the 5 GHz band
- 802.11g/Wi-Fi 3: 54 Mbps bandwidth on the 2.4 GHz band and backward compatibility with 802.11b
- 802.11n/Wi-Fi 4: 600 Mbps bandwidth on the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands
- 802.11ac/Wi-Fi 5: ~7 Gbps bandwidth on the 5 GHz band; calls for use of Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance (CSMA/CA)
- 802.11ax/Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E: 11 Gbps bandwidth on the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands
- 802.11be/Wi-Fi 7: 46 Gbps bandwidth on the 2.4, 5, and 6 GHz bands
New specifications with Wi-Fi 4/5/6:
- Channel bonding: aggregation of multiple adjacent WLAN channels
- Band steering: ability of WAP to direct, or steer, a device to a better-performing frequency
- Multiple input multiple output (MIMO): leveraging at least two receiving antennas and one transmitting antenna per band to improve RF signal quality and bandwidth
- Multi-user MIMO (MU-MIMO): improved MIMO that improves on a device-by-device basis
Wi-Fi 7:
- Superwide channel: 320 MHz-wide channel available only in the 6 GHz band
- Multi-link operation (MLO): ability to connect to and operate on multiple wireless bands, or links
- 4K quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM): modification, or modulation, of wireless signals to carry more data
- 512 compressed block acknowledgement (ACK): transmitters and receivers can compress 512 MAC protocol data units (MPDUs) into a single frame to acknowledge transmissions
- Multiple (multi) resource unit (RU) punching: allows unused portions of a wireless channel to be used by multiple clients
802.1x: Describes an implementation of port-based access control for wireless networks
802.3x: An Ethernet standard for flow control
## 9.4 Wireless network design
Site survey: evaluating RF behavior to achieve roaming, simplify association, and satisfy security requirements
Roaming: device's ability to maintain association
Association: device's ability to discover and connect to a WLAN
Wifi analyzer: software-based tool used during a site survey to evaluate RF behavior
Heat map: included with most wifi analyzers to visually represent a WLAN's coverage area
Service set: group of wireless devices 
- Independent basic service set (IBSS)/ad-hoc: without a WAP
- Basic service set (BSS): at least one WAP configured with a single SSID
- Extended service set (ESS): at least two WAPs configured with the same SSID
Service set identifier (SSID): logical identifier for a service set
Autonomous/unmanaged WAP: individually-managed WAP providing all WLAN services and functionality
Lightweight (LWAP)/managed WAP: centrally-managed WAP providing the WLAN services received from a WLC
Wireless LAN controller (WLC): centralized device used to control and configure multiple managed WAPs
Cellular network: wireless network composed of small geographical areas called cells, central towers, and wireless devices
- Time division multiple access (TDMA): Obsolete 2G technology
- Global system for mobile communications (GSM): European-developed protocol suite used by the second generation (2G) of wireless mobile communication.
- Code division multiple access (CDMA): underlying channel access method used by the third generation (3G) of wireless mobile communication.
- Long term evolution (LTE): wireless broadband communication standard used by the fourth generation (4G) of wireless mobile communication.
- Fifth generation (5G): latest wireless broadband communication standard used by cellular networks.
## 9.5 WLAN attacks
On-path attack: attacker eavesdrops and modifies the communications between two parties
Secure channel: guarantees data authenticity and confidentiality
Spoofing: user attempts to impersonate another user or device
Rogue AP: WAP that is in range of a WLAN, but does not belong to the entity controlling the WLAN
Evil twin: attack where the attacker presents a fake WAP and tricks the user's device into associating with it
Wireless intrusion prevention system (WIPS): WLAN monitoring device or service capable of detecting and preventing intrusions
Deauthentication: attack where a user's authentication is invalidated
Wardriving: Searching for wireless networks to map their locations and identify vulnerabilities

## 9.6 WLAN security
MAC filtering: controls WLAN access by comparing a wireless device's MAC address with a list
Pre-shared key (PSK): shared credential used for device authentication 
Extensible authentication protocol (EAP): framework for transporting different types of authentication protocols
Geofencing: using GPS or RFID technology to define a virtual perimeter for a geographic boundary
Captive portal: website user must open before WLAN association is granted
Wi-Fi protected access (WPA): 256-bit TKIP or AES encryption, message integrity, shared key
Wi-Fi protected access 2 (WPA2): always AES, complexity requirements of PSK are enhanced, EAP is available
Wi-Fi protected access 3 (WPA3): enhanced security through three modes:
- WPA3-Personal: smaller, usually personal WLANs where security resources are not as robust
- WPA3-Enterprise: multiple EAP methods, 128-bit authenticated encryption, 256-bit key derivation, and 128-bit management frame protection
- WPA3-Enterprise with 192-bit mode: minimum 192-bit baseline for cryptographic tools
Protected management frames (PMF): encrypts traditionally unencrypted management frames; required for WPA3-Enterprise
Opportunistic wireless encryption (OWE) : IETF standard ensuring the use of a cryptographic handshake between devices and open network
Simultaneous authentication of equals (SAE): IEEE authentication protocol used to protect against offline attacks, password-guessing, and wardriving
Wi-Fi enhanced open: Wi-Fi standard based on OWE focused on reducing the risks associated with an open network without discouraging their use
