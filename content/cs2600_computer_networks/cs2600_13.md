## 13.1: Troubleshooting methodology
1. Identify the problem.
2. Establish a theory of probable cause.
3. Test the theory to determine the cause.
4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and identify potential effects.
5. Implement the solution or escalate as necessary.
6. Verify full system functionality and, if applicable, implement preventive measures.
7. Document findings, actions, outcomes, and lessons learned.
A layer 1 problem is a networking problem involving a physical layer item. Ex: Hardware or network media.
A layer 2 problem is a networking problem involving a data link layer item. Ex: A switch or a MAC address.
A layer 3 problem is a networking problem involving a network layer item. Ex: A router or an IP address.
A layer 4 problem is a networking problem involving a transport layer item. Ex: TCP or a firewall.
Level 0 support involves self-support resources. Ex: A knowledge base article or wiki page.
Level 1 support involves basic troubleshooting for end-user problems. Ex: Account lockouts.
Level 2 support involves in-depth troubleshooting of a networking device. Ex: A switch or router.
Level 3 support involves subject matter experts and troubleshooting a large-scale problem. Ex: Disaster recovery.
Level 4 support involves support from a vendor or service provider.
