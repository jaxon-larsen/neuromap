### IEEE 802.3 Ethernet standard
100BASE-FX
- transmission speed/100 Mbps
- communication type (baseband/BASE)
- physical medium (fiber/FX, short-range MMF/SX, long-range MMF/LX, long-range SMF/LR)

### Multiplexing
Multiplexing: consolidating multiple digital signals into a single signal for transmission
Wavelength Division Multiplexing (WDM): laser lights multiplex signals onto fiber media
- Unidirectional
- Bidirectional
	- Course
		- 18 communication channels providing a 10 Gbps aggregate bandwidth
	- Dense
		- 192 communication channels providing a 19.2 Tbps aggregate bandwidth.

### Fiber Connectors
ANSI/TIA-604, or Fiber Optic Connector Intermateability Standards (FOCIS)
Types of fiber connectors
- Angled physical contact (APC)
	- eight-degree angled polished end and a green connector color
- Ultra-physical contact (UPC)
	- slightly curved polished end and a blue connector color
- Straight tip (ST) connector
	- fiber connector using a twist-on twist-off, or bayonet, connection method.
- Local connector (LC)
	- small form factor (SFF) fiber connector with a retaining tab connection method.
- Subscriber connector (SC)
	- square-shaped fiber connector with a push-on, pull-off connection method.
- Mechanical transfer registered jack (MTRJ) 
	- small-form factor (SFF) fiber connector, resembling an RJ45 connector, with a tab-locking connection method.
Return loss:  the amount of loss caused by reflected light inside a fiber cable
Insertion loss: the amount of optical power loss due to a fiber cable's length

### Optical Transceivers
Transceiver: converts one kind of signal into another
- Small form-factor pluggable (SFP) transceiver is an optical transceiver supporting up to 1000BASE Ethernet
- Enhanced small form-factor pluggable (SFP+) transceiver is an optical transceiver used with 10GBASE Ethernet deployments
- Quad small form-factor pluggable (QSFP) transceiver is an optical transceiver used to carry four 1000BASE Ethernet fiber channels for an aggregate bandwidth of 4 Gbps
- Enhanced quad small form-factor pluggable (QSFP+) transceiver is an optical transceiver used to carry four 10GBASE Ethernet fiber channels for an aggregate bandwidth of 40 Gbps

### Bounded Media Planning
Media distribution frame (MDF): wiring rack inside a demarc connecting equipment of premise and provider
Intermediate distribution frame (IDF): wiring rack connecting premise's locations to their MDF, for instance those on multiple floors
Network drop: wall outlet with a bounded media receptacle
These connect a network drop to a LAN:
- Patch panel/patch bay: many ports to connect its copper media
	- Multimedia patch/distribution panels also provide fiber media ports
- Punchdown block: numerous slots to punch down, or terminate, a network drop's copper wiring
	- 66 block: used with telephone services
	- 110 block: used with CAT5 and CAT5e cabling
	- Krone: European alternative to 110 block
	- Building industry cross-connect (BIX): used with CAT6 cabling and above
- Fiber distribution panel: numerous ports used to connect fiber media to a LAN and terminate it

### Bounded media construction tools
Cable crimper: crimp a connector to each cable end
Cable stripper: safely remove a bounded media segment's outer jacket
Punchdown tool: terminate a copper cable into a punchdown block slot
Wire map: helps construct a TP cable with the correct pin-to-pair assignment

### Quiz
Twists in TP prevent crosstalk in adjacent wire pairs
Single-mode cables can span longer distances than multimode
Fiber-optic cable is more fragile than UTP, but it can go farther, is immune to electromagnetic interference, and prevents ground loops because it is non-conductive
Plenum-rated cable has a fire-retardant jacket, and is often used in HVAC
PVC cables release toxic, thick smoke when they burn
Bus topology: if the main cable goes down, everything goes down
Don't use coax, use TP
