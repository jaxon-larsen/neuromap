## The CIA Triad
Confidentiality: Ensuring information is not made available to those unauthorized
Integrity: Ensuring the accuracy and consistency of data; it cannot be modified in an unauthorized matter
Availability: Ensuring that systems and data are available and usable when needed by those authorized

Authenticity: Being genuine and being able to be verified and trusted
Accountability: Actions of an entity should be able to be traced uniquely to that entity
Privacy: Right of individuals to control which of their information may be collected or stored

## DES
1. Starts with a 64-bit block of plaintext and permutes according to $IP$
2. Splits into two halves
3. Right half and a subkey are put into the F function:
	- 32-bit half expanded to 48 bits to match the subkey size
	- XORed with the subkey of that round
	- 48 bits are broken into eight 6-bit chunks and put into an S-box
		- The first and last bits combined tell you the row (0-3)
		- The middle four bits tell you the column (0-15)
	- Output is 4 bits, which are permuted according to a fixed table, resulting in 32 bits returned
4. The output is XORed with the left half
5. The halves are swapped, and the process repeats for 16 rounds
6. Bits are permuted according to $IP^{-1}$ to produce the ciphertext

## RSA
Key generation:
1. Select two prime numbers: $p$ and $q$
2. Modulus: $n=p\times q$
3. Euler's totient: $\phi(n)=(p-1)\times (q-1)$
4. Public exponent: $1 \lt e \lt \phi(n)$ and $e$ is coprime to $\phi(n)$, so their only common factor is 1
5. Private exponent: make $d \times e \mod\phi(n) = 1$
6. Public key: $\{e, n\}$
7. Private key: $\{d, n\}$
8. Message: $0\le M \lt n$
9. Ciphertext: $C=M^e\mod n$
10. Decrypted message: $M=c^d\mod n$

Message authentication code (MAC): Uses symmetric keys to ensure the only people who match have the same key
Digital signatures: Encrypt with private key that only you have so that everyone know it was you

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
1. Something you know: Passwords, PINs
2. Something you possess
3. Something you are (Biometrics)
4. Something you do: Typing patterns, voice rhythm

Diffie-Helman (DH): Protocol used for key exchange; allows parties to create a shared secret over a public channel
Digital Signature Standard (DSS): Federal standard that uses the Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA) to provide authenticity and integrity
Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC): Successor to RSA; same security with smaller key sizes
