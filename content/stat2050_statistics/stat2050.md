| Standard Deviation      | $s$              | $\sigma$             |
| Variance                | $s^2$            | $\sigma^2$           |

Z-score calculation:
$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$
## Variance & Standard Error
### Sum of Squares & Variance
Sum of squares ($SS$): $\sum^{n}_{i=1}(x_i-\bar{x})^2$
Sample variance: $s^2 = \frac{SS}{n-1}$
### Standard Error ($SE$) Formulas
Single mean: $SE = {\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}}$
Single proportion: $SE = \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}}$
Single count: $SE = \sqrt{n\times p \times (1-p)}$
Difference of means: $SE_{(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2)} = \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}$
## Distributions & Sampling Theory
### Sampling Distributions
Proportions: $\hat{p}\sim N(p, \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}})$
Means: $\bar{x}\sim N(\mu, \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}})$
T-distribution: $\frac{\bar{x}-\mu}{s/\sqrt{n}}\sim t_{df=n-1}$
### Linear Combinations of Normal Variables

If $A\sim N(\mu_1, \sigma_1)$ and $B\sim N(\mu_2, \sigma_2)$:
- General combination: $c_1A-c_2B \sim N(c_1\mu_1+c_2\mu_2, \sqrt{c_1^2\sigma_1^2+c_2^2\sigma_2^2})$
- Difference ($A-B$): $A-B \sim N(\mu_1-\mu_2, \sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2})$
### Rules & Requirements
Central limit theorem (Proportions): Applies when $n \times p \ge 10$ and $n \times (1-p) \ge 10$
T-distribution eligibility: If $n < 30$, ensure no extreme skewness or outliers
Degrees of freedom (two-sample): $df = min(n_1, n_2) - 1$
Independence: $P(A\cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$ if $A$ and $B$ are independent
Bootstrap sample: Must be the same size as the original sample, drawn with replacement
## Confidence Intervals
General form: $Point\ Estimate \pm ME$
Margin of error ($ME$): $ME = z^*\text{ or }t^*\times SE$
### Specific Intervals
95% rule of thumb: $\bar{x} \pm (2\times SE)$
Single proportion: $\hat{p}\pm z^*\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}$
Single mean: $\bar{x}\pm t^* {\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}}$
Difference of means: $(\bar{x_1}-\bar{x_2})\pm t^* \sqrt{{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}}+{\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}$
Difference of proportions: $(\hat{p_1}-\hat{p_2})\pm z^* \sqrt{{\frac{\hat{p_1}(1-\hat{p_1})} {n_1}}+{\frac{\hat{p_2}(1-\hat{p_2})} {n_2}}}$
### Critical Values ($z^*$)

| Confidence Level | z∗    |
| ---------------- | ----- |
| 90%              | 1.645 |
| 95%              | 1.960 |
| 98%              | 2.326 |
| 99%              | 2.576 |

### Sample Size Determination
To find $n$ for a specific $ME$:

$$n = (\frac{z^*\sigma}{ME})^2$$
## Hypothesis Testing
Null hypothesis ($H_0$): pre-held belief, status quo, or a statement of "no effect" (e.g., $H_0: \mu = \mu_0$ or $H_0: p = p_0$)
Alternative hypothesis ($H_a$): alternative belief; what you are trying to prove (e.g., $H_a: \mu \neq \mu_0$ or $H_a: p > p_0$)
The goal: to find evidence against $H_0$ in favor of $H_a$
$p$-value: measure of evidence against $H_0$, or the probability of seeing data as extreme as the sample when $H_0$ is true; a lower value is greater evidence against $H_0$
Statistical significance: have a p-value small enough that our result is unlikely to have occurred by chance if $H_0$ is true
Significance level: $\alpha$; commonly <u>0.05</u>, 0.10, or 0.01
If $p$-value < $\alpha$: we have statistical significance
- $p < \alpha$: reject $H_0$, accept $H_a$
- $p > \alpha$: fail to reject $H_0$, cannot accept $H_a$
- <b><u>NEVER</u></b> ACCEPT $H_0$!!!
Procedure
0. Question
1. State hypotheses
2. Determine $\alpha$
3. Take sample, get statistic
4. Find $p$-value
5. Make decision
6. Explain in context

|           |       | Decision      |                |
| --------- | ----- | ------------- | -------------- |
|           |       | Fail ($H_0$)  | Reject ($H_a$) |
| **Truth** | $H_0$ | :)            | Type I Error   |
|           | $H_a$ | Type II Error | :)             |
test statistic = $\frac{\text{sample statistic}-\text{hypothesized value}}{SE}$
### Single Proportion
Single categorical variable (single level)
$\hat{p}\sim N(p, \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}})$
$H_0: p = p_0$; $H_a: p ? p_0$
$$z = \frac{\hat{p}-p_0}{\sqrt{\frac{p_0(1-p_0)}{n}}}$$
$n\times p_0 \ge 10$; $n\times (1-p_0) \ge 10$
### Single Mean
Single quantitative variable
$H_0: \mu = \mu_0$; $H_a: \mu ? \mu_0$
$t = \frac{\bar{x}-\mu_0}{\sqrt{s^2/n}}$; $df = n - 1$
### Two Proportions (Confidence Interval)
Single categorical (one level) and categorical (two levels)
$H_o: p_1 - p_2 = 0$; $H_o: p_1 = p_2$; $H_a: p_1 ? p_2$
$$z = \frac{\hat{p_1}-\hat{p_2}}{\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p_1}(1-\hat{p_1})}{n_1}+\frac{\hat{p_2}(1-\hat{p_2})}{n_2}}}$$
### Two Proportions (Test)
$H_o: p_1 = p_2$
Pooled proportions:
$$\hat{p} = \frac{x_1 + x_2}{n_1 + n_2}$$
$$z = \frac{(\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2) - 0}{\sqrt{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})(\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2})}}$$
### Two Means
Single quantitative (one level) response and categorical (two levels) variable
$H_o: \mu_1 = \mu_2$; $H_a: \mu_1 ? \mu_2$
$$t=\frac{\bar{x}_1-\bar{x}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s^2_1}{n_1}+\frac{s^2_2}{n_2}}}$$
$df=min(n_1,n_2)-1$

### Paired Study
Where $d$ is the difference between the paired treatments: 
$H_o: \mu_d = 0$
$$s_d = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x}_d)^2}{n - 1}}$$
$SE = \frac{s_d}{\sqrt{n}}$
$t_d = \frac{\bar{x}_d}{SE}$
$ME = t^* \times SE$
Confidence interval: $\bar{x_d} \pm ME$
### Chi-Squared Test
For independence/association or goodness of fit
$$\chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i}$$
Test statistic: $\chi^2$
Observed frequencies: $O$
Expected frequencies: $E$

$\text{Expected count} = \frac{(\text{row total}) \times (\text{column total})}{\text{total total}}$
$\text{Expected counts must be} \ge 5$ 
$df = (\text{num rows} - 1) \times (\text{num cols} - 1)$

### Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)
$H_o: \mu_1 = \mu_2 ... = \mu_k$ $H_a: \text{At least one is different}$
#### Sum of Squares
SSG (Groups/Between): measures variability between group means
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_i(\bar{x}_i-\bar{x})^2 \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (\bar{x}_i-\bar{x})^2$$+ SSE (Error/Within): measures variability within each group
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} (n_i - 1)s_i^2 \quad \text{or} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (x_{ij} - \bar{x}_i)^2$$
= Overall (SSTotal): total variability
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (x_{ij}-\bar{x})^2$$

$k$: number of groups
$n$: total sample size

| Source                     | $df$                           | Sum of Squares                                                  | Mean of Squares       | $F$-statistic       |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------- |
| Groups (Model, Regression) | $k-1 (df_1 \text{ or d.f.N.})$ | $SSG=\sum_{i=1}^{n}n_i(\bar{x}_i-\bar{x})^2$                    | $MSG=\frac{SSG}{k-1}$ | $F=\frac{MSG}{MSE}$ |
| Error                      | $n-k (df_2\text{ or  d.f.D.})$ | $SSE=\sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (x_{ij}-\bar{x}_i)^2$      | $MSE=\frac{SSE}{n-k}$ |                     |
| Total                      | $n-1$                          | $SS_{Total}=\sum_{i=1}^{k} \sum_{j=1}^{n_i} (x_{ij}-\bar{x})^2$ |                       |                     |

$r^2 = \frac{SSG}{SS_{Total}}$
#### Requirements/Assumptions
1. Independent
2. Each group's values follow a normal distribution
3. Constant variance among groups
	- Spread of one group ($s_i$) cannot be twice or more of another
#### ANOVA $H_0$ Rejected (Post Hoc)
New estimate of population variance: $MSE$
Pooled standard deviation: $s_p = \sqrt{MSE}$
Standard Error for difference: $SE = \sqrt{\frac{MSE}{n_i} + \frac{MSE}{n_j}}$
Confidence interval for $\mu_i - \mu_j$:
$$\bar{x}_i \pm t^* \sqrt{\frac{MSE}{n_i}}$$
$$\bar{x}_i - \bar{x}_j \pm t^* \sqrt{\frac{MSE}{n_i}+\frac{MSE}{n_j}} $$
$$t=\frac{\bar{x}_i - \bar{x}_j}{\sqrt{\frac{MSE}{n_i}+\frac{MSE}{n_j}}} $$
Use $df=n-k$ for $t^*$
### Linear Regression
#### Correlation ($r$)
$$r = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (\frac{x_i - \bar{x}}{s_x}) (\frac{y_i - \bar{y}}{s_y})$$
$H_0: \rho = 0$ (no linear relationship)
$H_a: \rho \ne 0$ (linear relationship exists)
Test Statistic: $t = \frac{r\sqrt{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}$ with $df = n - 2$
#### The Regression Line
$H_0: b_1 = 0$
$H_a: b_1 \ne 0$
$$\hat{y} = b_0 + b_1x$$
Slope: $b_1 = r \frac{s_y}{s_x}$
Intercept: $b_0 = \bar{y} - b_1\bar{x}$
#### Variability & Fit
Coefficient of Determination ($r^2$): The percentage of variation in the response variable ($y$) explained by the linear model ($x$).
Residual ($e$): $y - \hat{y}$ (Observed value minus predicted value).
Residual Plot: Should show no clear pattern (random scatter) to validate the linear model.                
$t = \frac{b_1}{SE_{b_1}}$
$df=n-2$
$ME=t^* \times SE_{b_1}$
Confidence interval: $b_1 \pm t^* SE_{b1}$
#### Regression ANOVA
SSG: $\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\hat{y}_i-\bar{y})^2$
SSE: $\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2$

### Bayes' Rule
$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$$
$$P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)\times P(A)}{P(B)}$$
$$P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)\times P(A)}{P(B|A)\times P(A)+P(B|\neg A)\times P(\neg A)}$$
### Binomial Properties
Binomial random variable:
- **B - Binary:** Each trial must have exactly two possible outcomes, usually categorized as a "success" or a "failure."
- **I - Independent:** The outcome of one trial must not affect the outcome of any other trial.
- **N - Number:** There must be a **fixed** number of trials ($n$) determined _before_ you start the process.
- **S - Same:** The probability of success ($p$) must be the same for every single trial.
$$P\binom{n}{x}=\frac{n!}{x!(n-x)!}$$
$$P(X=x) = \binom{n}{x} p^x (1-p)^{n-x}$$
$$\mu = np$$
$$\sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)}$$
### Random Variables
$$\mu = \sum [x \cdot p(x)]$$
$$\sigma^2 = \sum [x^2 \cdot p(x)] - \mu^2$$
